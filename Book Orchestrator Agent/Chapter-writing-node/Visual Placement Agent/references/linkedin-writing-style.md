# LinkedIn-Derived Final Chapter Style

This reference governs only Artifact B, the final reader-facing chapter produced after the canonical Anshul-style chapter has already passed its own evaluation.

It does not govern Artifact A and does not replace the canonical `anshul-chapter-writing-4` skill.

## Voice

Write like a product leader explaining a shift they have actually worked through.

The tone should be:
- conversational but not casual;
- direct but not simplistic;
- analytical without sounding academic;
- confident where evidence is strong and calibrated where it is not;
- plain-English first, jargon second;
- concrete before abstract.

Avoid generic book prose, consultant filler, hype, motivational language, hashtags, emojis, engagement bait, and social-post calls to comment/share.

## Argument movement

Prefer this movement when it fits the canonical argument:

1. Start with a familiar assumption, operating model, or tension.
2. State the obvious first-order interpretation.
3. Turn with the second-order consequence: what changes once that assumption is no longer enough.
4. Explain the mechanism in plain English.
5. Ground it in the canonical example/evidence.
6. Land the product implication.

Do not force this template mechanically into every subsection. The chapter should feel like one unfolding argument, not a stack of mini-posts.

## Opening

- No generic `Introduction` heading.
- Open with 3–6 short paragraphs that establish the shift/tension quickly.
- Get to the chapter's real product problem before discussing mechanisms.
- Do not invent a personal anecdote that is absent from Artifact A.

## Paragraphs and rhythm

- Prefer short paragraphs, usually 1–3 sentences.
- One paragraph should carry one idea or turn.
- Mix short and medium sentences for natural rhythm.
- A single short sentence may be used sparingly as a landing line, but avoid stacks of one-liners.
- Do not preserve the canonical chapter's heavier bold/italic density just because Artifact A used it; Artifact B should read cleanly.

## Headings

- H1: chapter title.
- Main sections: H2 and numbered `1.`, `2.`, `3.` etc.
- Subsections: H3 only where the approved structure has them.
- Preserve approved `[TECH]` and `[WOW MOMENT]` markers.
- Prefer idea-led/question-led headings already approved by the blueprint; do not invent a new section taxonomy.

## Technical explanation

When technical vocabulary appears:
- explain the concept in plain language first;
- then name the technical term;
- show why it matters to a product decision;
- use the canonical evidence/example instead of a generic hypothetical when one exists.

## Evidence and citations

- Preserve Artifact A's factual meaning, statistics, source attribution, and caveats.
- Integrate evidence naturally into the argument rather than making the chapter read like a research report.
- Never strengthen a claim beyond the source.
- Never remove or swap a source solely for stylistic reasons.
- Do not add new research.

## Transitions

Transitions should explain causality: why the next section follows from the previous one.

Avoid mechanical phrases such as:
- `Now let's look at...`
- `Moving on...`
- `In the next section...`

Prefer a real argument bridge.

## Lists

Use bullets only when the content is genuinely a set, taxonomy, checklist, or comparison. Otherwise use prose.

## Humor

Sparse, dry humor is allowed only when it clarifies a point and already fits the author's voice. Never manufacture jokes to make the prose feel more human.

## Uncertainty

Use calibrated uncertainty where the canonical evidence is limited. Do not add vague hedging such as `arguably`, `in a sense`, or `it could be said` when the chapter has enough evidence to take a position.

## Counterargument

Preserve the chapter's strongest counterargument and give it a fair hearing. The final style should make the tension sharper, not erase it for momentum.

## Conclusion

Use 3–6 short paragraphs.

Return to the opening tension or changed operating model. State the second-order implication clearly. Land the approved thesis/punch line without a generic `the future is exciting` close.

If the canonical chapter includes reader-facing action questions approved as part of the ending, preserve them cleanly. Do not add social-media engagement prompts.

## Reader-facing cleanliness

Artifact B must not contain:
- evaluator divider blocks;
- evaluator findings;
- workflow notes;
- Chapter Writing Notes;
- Manuscript Handoff Package;
- Completeness Summary;
- Visual Review metadata;
- KEEP/EXCLUDE/PENDING fields;
- placement audit details.

Those belong in separate workflow artifacts.

## Precedence

For Artifact B only:
1. explicit human instruction for this chapter;
2. approved blueprint structure and fixed punch lines;
3. factual accuracy, evidence, citations, and caveats from Artifact A/Deep Research;
4. this LinkedIn-derived style reference;
5. generic nonfiction convention.

Style never overrides facts, citations, evidence scope, or approved structure.
