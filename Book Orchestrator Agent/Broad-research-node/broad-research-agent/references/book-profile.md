# Book Profile: Products of Tomorrow

## What This Book Is About

Products of Tomorrow is a nonfiction book about the fundamental transformation
happening in how software products are built, experienced, and trusted.

The central argument: the products we know today — static apps with fixed screens,
feature checklists, and passive interfaces — are ending. What replaces them will be
living systems that adapt to us, act for us, understand us emotionally, and earn the
right to stay in our lives through trust.

The book is organized around four pillars:

1. **Adaptive UX** — Products that shape-shift around us. Interfaces generated live,
   not pre-built. Context that goes beyond personalization — reading the room and
   reading the person. Products that grow with the user's goals and maturity over time.

2. **Agents** — Products that act, not just wait. The shift from passive tools to
   goal-driven doers. Swarms of specialized micro-agents working as one product.
   Machine-to-machine ecosystems. Products as economic actors in their own right.

3. **Emotional Intelligence** — Products that feel us and feel with us. Companions
   that listen, empathize, and encourage. Context-aware care that adjusts to emotional
   state. The shift from tools to teammates that nudge, celebrate, and check in.

4. **Trust** — The ultimate moat. As products take on life-level autonomy (money,
   health, kids, identity), trust becomes the deciding factor. Trust contracts, governance
   built in, and the thin line between magic and creepy.

## Scope

The book focuses on **personal life contexts** (Section 1): products in daily life —
fitness, education, finance, wellness, travel, calendar, and home. The professional
context (Section 2) exists as reference material but is **not the primary focus**.

## Core Thesis

We are moving from products as interfaces → products as actors, companions, and
partners. The DNA of a great product is shifting from features and usability to
adaptivity, agency, emotional intelligence, and trust. PMs who understand this shift
will define the next generation of products. Those who don't will be left building
yesterday's apps.

## Audience

**Primary audience: Product Managers** — mid-to-senior PMs at technology companies
who are thinking about the next wave of product design. They are technically literate
but not engineers. They understand product strategy, user behavior, and the language
of roadmaps, OKRs, and user research. They are curious, forward-looking, and
practically minded — they want to understand what's coming AND what to do about it.

**Secondary audience:** Founders, designers, and tech-forward business leaders who
care about where products are headed.

## What the Reader Should Walk Away With

- A mental model for why current products are structurally limited
- A clear framework (the four pillars) they can apply to their own product decisions
- Named concepts and vocabulary they can use in meetings and roadmap discussions
- A sense of excitement and urgency — this shift is already happening
- Practical signals: what to design differently, what questions to ask, what to watch for

## Tone and Purpose

This is not an academic textbook and not a how-to manual. It sits between a
thought-leadership book and a practical framework guide. It should feel like a
smart, curious PM friend who just spent six months researching the future of
products — and is now telling you everything they found over coffee.

The book respects the reader's intelligence without assuming deep technical knowledge.
It earns their time by being specific, concrete, and honest about what is real today
versus what is coming.

## What This Book Is NOT

- Not a technical implementation guide
- Not a history of AI or software
- Not a dystopian warning or utopian hype
- Not written for engineers or data scientists
- Not exhaustive — it is opinionated and curated
