# Synthesize Book Research

Read [the canonical contracts](../plan-book-research/references/output-formats.md) before drafting. Keep one shared reference.

## Inputs and purpose

Use a new fresh context, distinct from planner and research workers, with minimal artifact-based inputs and `fork_turns: "none"` or equivalent when supported. Read the supplied background, expert investigations, current Question Map, prior synthesis/gaps when applicable and book decisions. Disclose missing inputs or context limitations.

Build a coherent, evidence-informed picture of tomorrow, not a source compilation or generic forecast list. Integrate professional lenses: emerging product forms, human behavior, technological changes, economic incentives, new markets, dependencies, disagreements and second-order effects. Explain mechanisms and conditions. Preserve facts, signals, inference and speculation as distinct levels of support.

Use a **Question Map, never a Term Map or Query Map**. Preserve question/source links and propose meaningful new questions revealed by integration. Reapply the shared question gate to revised top-level questions. Do not mistake shared-source repetition for independent agreement.

## Synthesize and challenge

1. Check whether the supplied frame still fits the evidence. Briefly state any important assumption or serious alternative; keep the authorized scope unless the user changes it. Do not reopen settled decisions without new evidence.
2. Identify the strongest cross-perspective insights and their mechanisms. Show where experts reinforce each other, rely on the same evidence or disagree for substantive reasons. Group by ideas, not experts or source categories.
3. Develop plausible future product/interaction forms and scenarios where useful. Explain enabling conditions, who gains/loses, dependencies and what signals would distinguish alternatives. Avoid assigning unsupported probabilities or treating today's absence of adoption as proof of future impossibility.
4. Examine consequential second-order effects and surprising connections. Label original reasoning and show its evidence base. Do not claim these consequences were measured.
5. Preserve strong counterarguments, material uncertainty and observable conditions that would change the main view. Reassess overstated claims explicitly; keep prior evidence records and attach reasons for changed interpretations.
6. Connect insights to supplied Products of Tomorrow themes and possible chapter arguments, examples, openings or distinctions. Suggest only as many writing opportunities as the research earns.
7. Rank genuine unresolved gaps by their ability to change a conclusion, scenario or chapter. Return G/Q IDs, evidence needs and exit conditions for conditional targeted research; do not manufacture a second pass.

Use the flexible synthesis contract. Maintain deduplicated concepts/relationships only where they help explain dependencies; do not impose a seven-section taxonomy, fixed theme counts, compulsory chapter alternatives or a large completeness form.

Normally synthesize from supplied evidence. If a material claim requires a small provenance/disconfirmation check, use native search by default, follow the shared source gates and add its question/source links. Before any Exa call validate explicit authorization for this individual run and available tools; never activate it because synthesis needs stronger evidence. Return substantial retrieval needs to the planner rather than silently running another research round.

## Final checks and handoff

Apply the shared editorial pass. Give consequential reasoning space; remove repeated definitions, examples, caveats and conclusions. Use selective bold for the insights a writer should notice. Ensure each important future claim has an evidence-to-mechanism-to-implication chain or is explicitly speculative.

For **synthesis 1**, return the integrated narrative, map/relationship updates, source pointers and ranked gap handoff or a justified no-targeted-research decision.

For **final synthesis after targeted research**, use all accepted evidence to strengthen, weaken or reverse earlier views; return concise change reasons, updated question/gap statuses and residual uncertainty. If no targeted work was warranted, the planner may accept synthesis 1 as final after checks.

Verify citations, source independence, plausible alternatives, substantive counterarguments, chapter relevance and editorial quality. Do not claim a full source universe or settled future. Return exclusive artifact paths and a compact check/status record; the planner alone updates the running index and saves numbered native Google Docs.
