## One substantive primary-source note

**E01 / S02 — More autonomy must earn its complexity.** Erik S. and Barry Zhang's [December 19, 2024 engineering essay](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) distinguishes predetermined workflows from model-directed agents. Their reasoning is economic and operational: flexibility can improve performance, but adds latency, cost and failure opportunities. A simpler solution suffices when the task does not need discretionary execution. Their customer-service routing illustration sends different query categories to specialized downstream processes; its value comes from separation, not unrestricted autonomy.

For open-ended tasks, the authors describe a loop that observes tool results, adapts actions and can stop or seek human feedback. Coding and computer-use implementations illustrate this pattern. The rationale is that unknown steps require flexibility, while environmental feedback checks progress. The account also warns of compounding errors. Native search and opened passages verified these examples and their attribution.

**Researcher inference:** A personal agent could therefore become an orchestrator of differently bounded activities rather than one uniformly autonomous personality. A recurring appointment might follow a predictable process; resolving a new conflict might require interpretation, negotiation and a human choice. That scenario is constructed here, not reported user behavior. Its mechanism is conditional: if predictable work remains cheaper to verify, product designers could reserve discretion for exceptions. The consequence could be a visible division between routine execution and consequential decisions. Conversely, if flexible execution becomes inexpensive and consistently verifiable, the distinction may matter less to users even when it remains important internally.

For the writer, the useful question is where delegation changes the burden rather than simply increases capability. A chapter could contrast two ways to deliver the same intended outcome and ask who notices a mistake, who repairs it and whether the person saved effort overall. Those are proposed investigations, not study findings. This is practitioner reasoning, not a controlled household comparison; the page warns its tooling landscape has changed. Its argument can inform future possibilities without establishing consumer preference or present SDK behavior.
