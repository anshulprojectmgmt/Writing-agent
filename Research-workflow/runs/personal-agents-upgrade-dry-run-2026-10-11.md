This sample applies `plan-book-research`, `research-book-topic`, `synthesize-book-research` and their shared canonical contract. It is a format and provider-control demonstration, not a completed research workflow or an independent expert consensus.

**Frame.** Explore how personal agents could change everyday delegation, product value and human agency for the broad readership of *Products of Tomorrow*. Working horizon: the next three to five years, provisionally chosen for this sample; as-of/access date: 2026-10-11. “Personal agent” is a working concept: software acting across tasks and services on a person's behalf. The dry-run worker had no authorization to publish or write to Google Drive/GitHub; the parent task separately authorizes committing this sample with the upgrade.

## Background outline

- **Meaning and significance (BQ01):** What changes when a person delegates an outcome instead of operating each application? Separate conversation, predefined workflows and adaptable action; the distinction affects expectations and control.
- **Technical and product forces (BQ02):** How could tools, contextual memory, environmental feedback and service access make delegation more useful? S001 informs architectural reasoning, not a present-day feature inventory.
- **Human and economic forces (BQ03):** How could users decide what to entrust, providers price outcomes, and service owners negotiate access? These are inquiry directions, not established trends in this sample.
- **Uncertainty (BQ04):** Which tasks permit reliable verification, and which require judgment about conflicting preferences? Investigate the conditions under which broader delegation becomes acceptable, rather than assume universal autonomy.

## Substantive source note: bounded autonomy as a product choice

[Anthropic's *Building effective agents*](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) distinguishes fixed orchestration from model-directed action. Its argument is conditional: flexibility can justify agent loops, but latency, cost and compounding errors favor simpler designs where sufficient. Environmental feedback, stopping conditions and human checkpoints make delegated work assessable. Coding illustrates why feedback matters: tests expose errors, while human review still judges wider requirements. This is practitioner guidance, not a controlled comparison proving consumer demand.

**Researcher inference:** personal agents could offer different delegation boundaries by task. Verifiable activities might permit longer autonomous runs; ambiguous preferences might call for review. The mechanism is a difference in available feedback, not an assumed universal appetite for autonomy. This suggests a chapter about how products allocate judgment. Household suitability remains untested here.

**Date honesty:** the page is labeled **December 19, 2024**. Its current notice says the tooling landscape has changed and points to Claude Managed Agents materials. The notice has no visible date. This live, edited page is not a frozen 2024 snapshot or evidence of current adoption.

## Example human-facing report structure

A full report could lead with the most consequential shift: **delegation boundaries may become part of the product's value proposition**. It would develop the evidence and mechanism before discussing possible interfaces, incentives and beneficiaries; examine contrasting futures such as bounded task specialists and broader personal intermediaries; preserve disagreements about convenience, accountability and market power; and close with earned chapter opportunities and observable conditions that would change the view. These are proposed report directions, not findings from six completed expert investigations. Process records belong in a compact appendix.

One consequential gap worth considering in a full run is G001/Q002–Q004: whether people actually accept and sustain differentiated autonomy across everyday tasks. Needed evidence: longitudinal firsthand use with failure/recovery observations and explicit delegation choices. Owner: UX Researcher. Exit condition: enough observed behavior to strengthen, weaken or split the bounded-autonomy hypothesis. No targeted pass is launched in this dry run.
