# Research Book Topic

Read [the canonical contracts](../plan-book-research/references/output-formats.md) before retrieval and drafting. Resolve this from the skill directory; do not duplicate it.

## Research stance

**Common expert mission:** Explore and define the future of the topic through your professional perspective. Investigate what is happening today, what is changing, plausible future states, shaping forces and new products, behaviors, experiences, markets and consequences.

Use the shared **future-first reasoning guide**, not just a current feature inventory: explain how a present signal and a changing force could produce a future state, what follows from it and what could lead elsewhere. Include second-order consequences and evidence-informed imagination. These are thinking dimensions, not compulsory headings. Keep facts, emerging signals, source interpretations, researcher inference and speculation distinguishable. Follow supplied audience, scope, time horizon and frozen decisions.

Use a **Question Map, never a Term Map or Query Map**. In background mode, start with lightweight guiding prompts; no detailed initial map is required. Give retained sources a link to at least one guiding question, then transfer those links when the expert map is established. In expert/targeted modes, apply the shared question gate, preserve Q IDs and develop supporting questions as evidence warrants. Search strings are temporary operations beneath questions. Review the map after every pass; record added, revised, merged or retired questions without losing source links.

Work in a fresh separate context with artifact handoffs when supported. Write only assigned artifacts, not the planner's running index. Route a full workflow to `plan-book-research`; disclose any context-isolation limitation.

## Modes

- **Background:** explain meaning/significance, developments, changing forces, uncertainty and promising expert dimensions. Aim for enough breadth to select experts, with concrete examples and citations. Usually up to 1,000–1,500 words when warranted; shorter is acceptable. Do not create fifty findings or a detailed initial map.
- **Expert:** use **Level 1 — Generic professional role**, or **Level 2 — Domain-specific professional role** only when justified by the topic/background. Preserve the generic parent and specialization rationale (for example, Economist → Labor Economist for employment effects). Never use a company identity. Read the background, its examples, source notes and question-generation rationale before retrieval. Start from broad future questions, then deepen mechanisms and cases. A current feature inventory or narrow use case must not consume the whole professional perspective.
- **Targeted:** investigate consequential G/Q gaps and exit conditions. Read prior evidence first, seek missing/disconfirming observations and return what changed. Attempt all ten rungs for the gap questions; vary depth without skipping a rung or repeating earlier findings.

| 1 | Consulting & Analyst | Market shifts, survey findings, economics and organizational adoption |
| 2 | VC / Investor Research | Emerging business models, ecosystems, incentives and competitive possibilities |

**Mandatory coverage, adaptive depth.** Every rung requires an actual question-led search or inspection of a potentially relevant source/channel. A relevance judgment alone is not an attempt. Keep all ten rung rows in the traceability layer, recording the operation/date and useful finding, no useful result, blocked access or unavailable tool. For a blocked channel, try a reasonable accessible alternative within the authorized provider; if still blocked, preserve that limitation. Never mark an unattempted rung complete.

Go deepest where evidence or intellectual value is strongest. A rung with no useful result can end after a reasonable real attempt; do not fabricate findings, duplicate claims or force irrelevant sources into the reading layer. There is no five-findings-per-rung or fifty-findings requirement. Academic/research and general/recent news are mandatory like every other rung.

### Ground floor and blind spots

Ask **what products with real users already do some version of this, even partially or under another name**. Prefer named GenAI products and verified demonstrations/deployments; separate availability, scale and outcomes. Include enough useful examples to understand the starting point, without a product-count quota.

Then ask **what major product, company, platform, pattern or user problem an experienced professional would immediately notice is missing**. For a material suspected omission, run a targeted question-led check. Put useful results under their substantive theme and source category; do not create a new checklist stage or pad the report. Neither check is the final future argument.

Prioritize primary company/product blogs, documentation, founder writing, keynotes and firsthand practitioner accounts for product facts and strategic signals. Use original studies for their measured findings, analytical essays for arguments, and reporting/newsletters for discovery, critique and original observations. Explore credible unconventional perspectives and serious counterarguments. Company ambition is not adoption evidence; category prestige is not proof of quality.

Use provider relevance ranking for initial discovery, then apply three lightweight gates:
- **Relevance:** does the source materially help answer a research question, rather than merely mention its topic?
- **Intellectual value:** does it add original reasoning, credible evidence/observations, a useful future possibility, concrete mechanism/example, serious counterargument or conceptual distinction?
- **Evidence quality:** what is verified fact, firsthand observation, measured research, interpretation or speculation, and what scope/method/incentive limits its use?

Internally classify sources considered for use as **Deep Read / Selective Read / Skip**; keep a short rationale for material decisions, not a score or evaluation card for every result. A low-ranked primary source or serious counterargument may deserve retention. Open promising sources beyond snippets. Deep-read sources that materially shape an insight; read relevant passages for supporting facts. Note inaccessible pages and verify dates. In comparison mode, search equivalent questions, deduplicate URLs/underlying claims and compare usefulness, not just hit counts.

Distinguish announcement, demonstration, pilot, availability, adoption and measured outcomes. Preserve publication and observation dates separately; access date is not freshness. Corroborate broad claims of impact, demand or scale. Multiple reports repeating one announcement are one underlying source. Allocate stable S/E IDs in the assigned namespace and associate every retained source with a question.

## Write substantive notes

Organize the main report by ideas and themes. A major research note must preserve these six dimensions:
1. **Central idea:** what the author is actually arguing, in readable language—not only the headline conclusion.
2. **Context and underlying reasoning:** the motivating problem/observation, why the author believes the idea, and supporting evidence or causal argument.
3. **Concrete illustrations:** useful experiments, demonstrations, deployment observations, examples or results, bounded to their actual scope.
4. **Future implications:** what might follow for products, experience, behavior, technology, markets or society; explain the mechanism and distinguish source forecast from researcher inference.
5. **Writer's value:** the argument, narrative opening, surprising example, distinction, counterargument or connection to another book theme that a writer can use.
6. **Limitations:** material uncertainty, disagreement, source incentives, evidence gaps and conditions under which the implication would change.

Develop these in coherent prose, not six compulsory headings per article. Rich sources deserve enough paragraphs to preserve their thinking; minor facts may need only a sentence. Do not mechanically expand every article to the same length, flatten important reasoning into one-line claims or repeat the same argument across sources. A note passes only if a writer can understand why the idea matters and how it was reasoned, not just quote its conclusion.

Preserve important disagreements and use counterevidence to qualify scenarios. Label your own connections and forecasts as interpretation/speculation. Do not inflate a vendor assertion into independent proof or flatten an insightful article into a one-line claim. Cite material claims inline; keep evidence IDs and search logs in the compact traceability layer.

Apply the shared editorial pass: space by importance, meaningful bold emphasis, no generic introductions, repeated caveats, forced headings, word-count padding or explanations of trivial points. Compression must preserve causal reasoning and uncertainty.
